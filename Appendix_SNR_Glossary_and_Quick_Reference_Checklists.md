# Appendix: SNR Glossary and Quick-Reference Checklists

## A. How to Use This Appendix

Use this appendix during chart preparation, trade planning, execution, and review. It is a quick-reference tool, not a replacement for the full explanations in the main chapters. If a checklist conflicts with your written trading plan, your tested plan takes priority.

These checklists are educational. They do not guarantee profitable results, and they should be adapted to your instrument, broker, timeframe, risk tolerance, and testing evidence.

## B. SNR Glossary

### Acceptance
Price remains and closes beyond a level, suggesting the market is accepting prices on the new side rather than merely probing with a wick.

### ATR
Average True Range, a volatility measure used to compare zone width, stop buffers, and recent price movement.

### Break of Structure (BOS)
A break that confirms continuation of the current structural direction.

### Bullish Order Block
In SMC/ICT terminology, the last bearish candle or small bearish cluster before a strong bullish impulse; often overlaps with an SNR support zone.

### Candlestick Body
The area between a candle's open and close. It generally represents the price range accepted by the market during that period.

### Confluence
Agreement among multiple independent factors at a similar price and time.

### Discount
The lower half of a defined trading range, where ICT-style analysis generally favors buying when structure supports that direction.

### Engulfing Candle
A candle whose body fully covers the previous candle's body, suggesting a decisive shift in short-term control.

### Equilibrium
The midpoint of a defined range, often associated with the 50% level.

### Fair Value Gap (FVG)
A three-candle price-delivery imbalance in which the first and third candle ranges do not fully overlap.

### Fresh Zone
An SNR zone with no prior retest after formation.

### Higher High (HH)
A swing high above the previous swing high.

### Higher Low (HL)
A swing low above the previous swing low.

### Higher Timeframe (HTF)
The timeframe used to establish broad market bias and major zones, commonly Daily or Weekly in this book.

### Liquidity Sweep
A move beyond a known high or low that triggers clustered orders before price reverses or changes direction.

### Lower High (LH)
A swing high below the previous swing high.

### Lower Low (LL)
A swing low below the previous swing low.

### Lower Timeframe (LTF)
The timeframe used to time entries after price reaches a higher-timeframe zone, commonly M15 or M5.

### Market Structure Shift (MSS)
A break against the prior structural direction that warns the existing trend may be ending.

### MSNR
Market Structure plus Support and Resistance: the framework that filters SNR zones through structural context.

### MTF
Multi-timeframe analysis using separate timeframes for bias, confirmation, and execution.

### Premium
The upper half of a defined trading range, where ICT-style analysis generally favors selling when structure supports that direction.

### Rejection
Price briefly enters or exceeds an area and then closes away from it, showing that the move was not accepted.

### Resistance
A price zone where selling pressure has previously overwhelmed buying pressure.

### RBS
Resistance Becomes Support after a decisive break and successful retest from above.

### SBR
Support Becomes Resistance after a decisive break and successful retest from below.

### Sell-Side Liquidity
Orders commonly clustered below swing lows, including stops from long positions and breakout sell orders.

### SNR
Support and Resistance: price zones where buyer/seller imbalance has previously caused a reaction.

### Support
A price zone where buying pressure has previously overwhelmed selling pressure.

### Sweep High/Low
The extreme price reached during a liquidity sweep; it often becomes a logical invalidation point.

### Untested Zone
Another name for a fresh zone.

### Wick
The portion of a candle beyond its body, showing the highest or lowest price reached during the period and often indicating rejection.

## C. Daily Market-Preparation Checklist

### 1. Environment

- [ ] Confirm instrument, broker, and current trading session.
- [ ] Check spread, liquidity, and platform connectivity.
- [ ] Review the economic calendar and high-impact events.
- [ ] Note unusual volatility, gaps, or market conditions.

### 2. Higher-Timeframe Analysis

- [ ] Mark major Weekly/Daily swing highs and lows.
- [ ] Label structure as HH/HL, LH/LL, or range.
- [ ] Identify the current HTF bias.
- [ ] Mark major support and resistance zones.
- [ ] Remove irrelevant or overcrowded zones.
- [ ] Note the levels that would invalidate the bias.

### 3. Zone Qualification

- [ ] Was the zone formed by a meaningful buyer/seller handoff?
- [ ] Did price depart with a clear impulse?
- [ ] Is the zone aligned with a swing point or structural event?
- [ ] Is it fresh or within the permitted test count?
- [ ] Is the zone width reasonable for timeframe and volatility?
- [ ] Is it support, resistance, SBR, or RBS?

## D. MSNR and Confluence Checklist

- [ ] Does the zone agree with HTF structure?
- [ ] Is the setup trend-aligned or supported by a confirmed MSS?
- [ ] Does MTF structure confirm the approach?
- [ ] Is there a trendline intersection?
- [ ] Is there a rejection, engulfing, or handoff candle?
- [ ] Did price sweep relevant liquidity?
- [ ] Is an FVG located at or near the zone?
- [ ] Is the zone in a favorable premium/discount location?
- [ ] What is the confluence score?
- [ ] Has this score threshold been tested rather than chosen emotionally?

## E. Buy-at-Support Checklist

- [ ] HTF structure is bullish or price is at the bottom of a valid range.
- [ ] Support zone is qualified and properly drawn.
- [ ] Zone is fresh or within the tested freshness limit.
- [ ] MTF approach is corrective, not an invalidating bearish shift.
- [ ] Price has entered the zone.
- [ ] A bullish trigger has closed: rejection, engulfing, handoff, or sweep reversal.
- [ ] Entry price is recorded.
- [ ] Stop is below structural invalidation and the zone's outer boundary.
- [ ] First target is a logical swing high or opposing zone.
- [ ] Second target is defined, if applicable.
- [ ] Position size is calculated from monetary risk.
- [ ] Spread, news, and correlation conditions are acceptable.

## F. Sell-at-Resistance Checklist

- [ ] HTF structure is bearish or price is at the top of a valid range.
- [ ] Resistance zone is qualified and properly drawn.
- [ ] Zone is fresh or within the tested freshness limit.
- [ ] MTF approach is corrective, not an invalidating bullish shift.
- [ ] Price has entered the zone.
- [ ] A bearish trigger has closed: rejection, engulfing, handoff, or sweep reversal.
- [ ] Entry price is recorded.
- [ ] Stop is above structural invalidation and the zone's outer boundary.
- [ ] First target is a logical swing low or opposing zone.
- [ ] Second target is defined, if applicable.
- [ ] Position size is calculated from monetary risk.
- [ ] Spread, news, and correlation conditions are acceptable.

## G. Breakout-and-Retest Checklist

- [ ] The original zone is clearly established.
- [ ] Price closes decisively beyond the zone.
- [ ] Breakout has meaningful body size and follow-through.
- [ ] Breakout agrees with HTF structure or confirms an MSS.
- [ ] Price returns to test the broken zone, if a retest occurs.
- [ ] The zone is treated as RBS or SBR.
- [ ] A directional rejection or handoff confirms the retest.
- [ ] Stop is beyond the retest zone and invalidation point.
- [ ] Target is based on the next structural zone or liquidity objective.
- [ ] The entry has not been chased after excessive extension.

## H. Risk-Management Checklist

- [ ] Maximum monetary risk is defined before entry.
- [ ] Stop distance is measured from actual entry to invalidation.
- [ ] Broker contract size and tick value are confirmed.
- [ ] Position size includes spread, commission, and expected slippage.
- [ ] Combined correlated exposure remains within limits.
- [ ] Daily and weekly loss limits have not been reached.
- [ ] The trade does not require an artificially tight stop.
- [ ] The target is logical and offers acceptable tested expectancy.
- [ ] Management rules are written before the order is placed.
- [ ] No revenge, FOMO, or recovery-based sizing is influencing the decision.

## J. One-Page Execution Workflow

1. Establish Daily/Weekly structure.
2. Mark only major SNR zones.
3. Select the zone aligned with the HTF bias.
4. Check freshness, departure, width, and confluence.
5. Wait for price to reach the zone.
6. Confirm the MTF approach has not invalidated the thesis.
7. Move to M15/M5 only for entry timing.
8. Wait for the predefined trigger to close.
9. Calculate stop, target, position size, and total risk.
10. Check news, spread, correlation, and platform conditions.
11. Place the order only if every mandatory rule passes.
12. Manage according to the written plan.
13. Record the trade and screenshot immediately after completion.
14. Review patterns weekly and statistics using a meaningful sample.

## K. Final Principles

- SNR is a zone where order-flow imbalance previously produced a reaction, not a magic line.
- Market structure determines which zones deserve priority.
- Freshness, departure quality, and confluence help rank zones.
- Multi-timeframe analysis assigns one job to each timeframe.
- A zone touch is not an entry; confirmation and risk control are required.
- A losing trade can be a correct trade if the plan was followed.
- A profitable trade can be a dangerous trade if it broke the rules.
- No strategy eliminates uncertainty; risk management makes uncertainty survivable.
- Test every rule before trusting it with meaningful capital.
- Protect capital, execute consistently, and let a sufficiently large sample reveal the edge.
