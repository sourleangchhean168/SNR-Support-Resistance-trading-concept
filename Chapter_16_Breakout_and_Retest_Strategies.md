# Chapter 16: Breakout and Retest Strategies

## 16.1 What Makes a Breakout Tradable

A breakout occurs when price moves decisively beyond an established SNR zone. However, not every move beyond a level is a genuine breakout. Some are wick-based liquidity sweeps (Chapter 13), false breaks, or temporary news spikes. This chapter provides a structured way to distinguish a tradable breakout from a trap and then use the subsequent retest for a lower-risk entry.

## 16.2 Genuine Breakout vs. False Break

A higher-quality breakout usually has several characteristics:

- A candle **closes decisively** beyond the zone, rather than merely wicking through it.
- The breakout candle has meaningful body size relative to recent candles.
- Follow-through appears in the next candle or two instead of immediate rejection back into the zone.
- The breakout agrees with higher-timeframe market structure or produces a clearly confirmed MSS.
- The move is not solely the result of a temporary spread expansion or an unplanned news spike.

A false break typically shows a wick beyond the zone, a close back inside it, and no sustained follow-through. In SMC/ICT language, this may be a liquidity sweep rather than true acceptance beyond the level.

## 16.3 The Breakout-Retest Sequence

A standard breakout-retest setup follows this sequence:

1. Price approaches an established SNR zone.
2. Price closes beyond the zone with convincing momentum.
3. The original zone changes role: broken resistance can become support (RBS), while broken support can become resistance (SBR), as explained in Chapter 9.
4. Price retraces toward the broken zone.
5. The retest produces a rejection or lower-timeframe handoff in the breakout direction.
6. Entry occurs after confirmation, with invalidation beyond the opposite side of the zone.

The retest is an opportunity, not a requirement. Some breakouts continue without returning, and chasing those moves usually creates poor risk-to-reward conditions.

## 16.4 Long Breakout and Retest

For a bullish breakout:

- Resistance must be broken with a decisive close above the zone.
- Price should hold above the zone or return to test it from above.
- A bullish rejection, bullish engulfing candle, or bullish two-candle handoff confirms RBS.
- Enter long after confirmation.
- Place the stop below the retest zone's outer boundary.
- Target the next significant resistance or liquidity pool above.

## 16.5 Short Breakout and Retest

For a bearish breakout:

- Support must be broken with a decisive close below the zone.
- Price should remain below the zone or return to test it from underneath.
- A bearish rejection, bearish engulfing candle, or bearish two-candle handoff confirms SBR.
- Enter short after confirmation.
- Place the stop above the retest zone's outer boundary.
- Target the next significant support or sell-side liquidity below.

## 16.6 Multi-Timeframe Confirmation

Use the MTF process from Chapter 11:

- HTF establishes whether the breakout aligns with the broader trend or represents a genuine structural shift.
- H4/H1 confirms acceptance beyond the zone and helps refine the retest area.
- M15/M5 supplies the entry trigger after price returns to the zone.

Avoid trading an LTF breakout that directly contradicts a strong HTF zone unless the higher timeframe also shows a confirmed structural break.

## 16.7 Stop Placement and Risk-to-Reward

The stop belongs beyond the retest zone, not at the original breakout candle's midpoint. If the stop must be extremely wide, skip the trade rather than compressing the stop artificially. Calculate position size from the distance between entry and stop, then check whether the next logical target offers an acceptable reward relative to that risk.

Do not move the target farther away simply to manufacture a favorable ratio. The target must be based on real structure, an opposing SNR zone, or a clearly defined liquidity objective.

## 16.8 Worked Example: XAUUSD Bearish Breakout

XAUUSD has supported between $2,350 and $2,356 several times. Daily structure is bearish, and H4 closes decisively at $2,342 below the zone. Price then rallies back to $2,353. On M15, a bullish candle is followed by a strong bearish candle that closes below $2,350, confirming that former support is now resistance.

- Entry: short after the bearish confirmation.
- Stop: above the retest zone's outer boundary, for example near $2,361 after checking current volatility.
- Target 1: the prior swing low.
- Target 2: the next significant Daily support zone.

The setup is valid because the break, retest, role reversal, HTF bias, and LTF confirmation agree. If price had merely wicked below $2,350 and closed back above it, the event would be treated as a possible liquidity sweep rather than a confirmed bearish breakout.

## 16.9 Common Breakout Mistakes

- Entering on the first candle that touches or slightly pierces the zone.
- Treating a wick as proof of acceptance beyond the level.
- Chasing a breakout after price has already traveled too far from the zone.
- Ignoring scheduled news and abnormal volatility.
- Placing the stop inside the retest zone.
- Assuming every breakout must produce a retest.

## 16.10 Chapter Summary

- A tradable breakout requires decisive closure, follow-through, and structural context.
- The preferred entry is often the confirmed retest, where the old zone changes role.
- A wick beyond a zone followed by a close back inside may be a liquidity sweep, not a breakout.
- MTF analysis prevents low-timeframe breaks from being traded against stronger higher-timeframe structure.
- Stops, position size, and targets must be based on actual zone geometry and market structure.
