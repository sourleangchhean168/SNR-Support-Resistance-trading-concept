# Chapter 10: Market Structure + SNR (MSNR)

## 10.1 Opening Part 3

Part 2 gave you the tools to identify SNR zones in isolation. Part 3 combines those zones with market structure (Chapter 4) into a unified framework called **MSNR — Market Structure + Support and Resistance**. MSNR is not a separate strategy from everything you've learned; it's the synthesis of SNR mechanics with structural context, and it forms the backbone of every entry technique in Part 4.

## 10.2 Why SNR Alone Isn't Enough

A perfectly marked, fresh SNR zone (Chapters 5–8) can still fail if it contradicts the prevailing market structure. Recall from Chapter 4 that in a strong uptrend, resistance zones are lower-probability trades because you're fighting dominant buying pressure. MSNR formalizes this by requiring every SNR-based trade idea to pass a structural check before being considered valid.

## 10.3 The MSNR Framework — Three-Step Filter

1. **Determine HTF structure** (Chapter 4) — is the Daily/H4 chart in an uptrend (HH/HL), downtrend (LH/LL), or range?
2. **Locate the nearest significant SNR zone** in the direction structure favors — a support zone in an uptrend, a resistance zone in a downtrend, or either boundary in a range.
3. **Check zone quality** using Chapters 5–8 criteria: freshness (Ch.7), proper zone construction (Ch.8), and confluence with a swing point or MSS (Ch.4).

Only setups that pass all three steps qualify as high-probability MSNR trades. This filter alone eliminates a large share of the low-quality setups that trip up beginners who trade SNR zones in isolation without structural context.

## 10.4 MSNR in an Uptrend

In a confirmed uptrend (HH/HL sequence), MSNR prioritizes buying at support zones that form at or near a Higher Low. The logic: the market has already demonstrated buyers are in control (structure), and now price is returning to a zone (SNR) where that same buying pressure previously originated. This is the highest-probability MSNR setup type, and it's the model used in most of the buy-setup examples in Chapter 14.

## 10.5 MSNR in a Downtrend

Mirror logic applies: in a confirmed downtrend (LH/LL sequence), MSNR prioritizes selling at resistance zones forming at or near a Lower High. Attempting to buy support zones during a strong downtrend — even a "textbook" fresh zone — is explicitly discouraged under the MSNR framework unless there's clear evidence of an emerging MSS (Chapter 4) signaling the downtrend may be ending.

## 10.6 MSNR in a Range

In a range, structure doesn't favor either direction strongly, so MSNR treats both the top and bottom of the range as tradable zones — sell at range resistance, buy at range support — until a confirmed breakout or MSS shifts the market into a trending state. This is the one context where counter-directional SNR trades (selling resistance, buying support in the same range) are both considered valid under MSNR, because there is no dominant trend to fight.

## 10.7 Combining MSNR with Role Reversal

Chapter 9's role-reversal concept slots naturally into MSNR: a role-reversal zone that also aligns with the prevailing HTF structure is considered an especially strong MSNR setup. For example, if a downtrend causes price to break a support zone (confirming LH/LL structure) and then retest that broken zone as new resistance, you have simultaneous confirmation from structure, SNR, and role reversal — three independent signals agreeing, which is one of the highest-conviction setup types in this entire book.

## 10.8 Worked Example

XAUUSD Daily shows a clear downtrend: LH at $2,420, LL at $2,340, LH at $2,395 (lower than $2,420), currently pulling back toward a resistance zone at $2,370–$2,376 that also happens to be a role-reversal zone (former support that broke in the prior leg down). Applying the MSNR three-step filter: (1) structure is a confirmed downtrend, (2) the zone is a resistance zone in the direction structure favors, (3) the zone is fresh since breaking and aligns with the Lower High sequence. This passes all three MSNR criteria and would be flagged as a high-probability setup, to be executed using the sell-side rules covered in Chapter 15.

## 10.9 Chapter Summary

- MSNR combines market structure (Chapter 4) with SNR zone quality (Chapters 5–8) into a single three-step filter.
- Trend-aligned zones (support in uptrends, resistance in downtrends) are prioritized; counter-trend zones require extra caution.
- In ranges, both boundaries are tradable until a breakout or MSS shifts the market into a trend.
- Role-reversal zones that also align with structure represent some of the highest-conviction MSNR setups.
