# Chapter 4: Market Structure Fundamentals

## 4.1 Why Market Structure Comes Before SNR Marking

You cannot correctly mark an SNR zone without first understanding market structure. A support zone in an uptrend behaves differently from a support zone in a downtrend counter-rally, and mistaking one for the other is one of the most common reasons beginners lose money on otherwise "textbook" SNR setups. This chapter formalizes the swing-point concepts introduced briefly in Chapter 2.

## 4.2 Swing Highs and Swing Lows — Precise Definition

- A **swing high** is formed when a candle's high is higher than the highs of the candles immediately before and after it.
- A **swing low** is formed when a candle's low is lower than the lows of the candles immediately before and after it.

In practice, traders often use a 3-candle or 5-candle fractal (comparing the middle candle to two or more candles on each side) to filter out minor noise. For SNR purposes, always confirm swing points on your chosen analysis timeframe (Daily/H4, per Chapter 2) — swing points on M5 are far too noisy to build reliable zones from.

## 4.3 The Four Structural Labels

Every swing point gets labeled relative to the previous one of the same type:

- **HH (Higher High)** — a swing high above the previous swing high.
- **HL (Higher Low)** — a swing low above the previous swing low.
- **LH (Lower High)** — a swing high below the previous swing high.
- **LL (Lower Low)** — a swing low below the previous swing low.

## 4.4 Classifying Trend State

- **Uptrend** — a consistent sequence of HH and HL. Buyers are in control; pullbacks should find support at higher lows.
- **Downtrend** — a consistent sequence of LH and LL. Sellers are in control; rallies should find resistance at lower highs.
- **Range** — swing highs and lows stay roughly at the same levels (no clear HH/HL or LH/LL sequence). Price oscillates between a ceiling and floor.

Knowing the current trend state tells you which SNR zones deserve more weight. In an uptrend, support zones (buy zones) at higher lows are generally higher probability than counter-trend resistance zones. In a downtrend, the reverse is true. This single rule prevents a huge percentage of beginner losses — fighting the trend at a resistance zone during a strong uptrend is one of the fastest ways to get run over.

## 4.5 Market Structure Shift (MSS) / Break of Structure (BOS)

Two events signal that trend state may be changing:

- **Break of Structure (BOS)** — price breaks beyond the most recent swing high (in an uptrend) or swing low (in a downtrend), confirming the existing trend is continuing.
- **Market Structure Shift (MSS)** — price breaks the most recent swing low in an uptrend (or swing high in a downtrend), signaling the prior trend may be ending and a reversal could be underway.

This distinction matters enormously for SNR trading: an SNR zone that forms *after* an MSS carries very different implications than one that formed mid-trend. Chapter 10 (Market Structure + SNR) builds an entire trading framework around this interaction, so make sure this distinction is second nature before continuing.

## 4.6 Structure on Multiple Timeframes

Market structure on the Daily chart might show a clear uptrend, while the H1 chart nested inside it shows a temporary downtrend (a pullback). This is completely normal and expected — it's the basis of the multi-timeframe approach formalized in Chapter 11. As a rule for this book:

- **Higher timeframe (HTF) structure** determines your directional bias.
- **Lower timeframe (LTF) structure** is used to time entries once price reaches an HTF SNR zone that aligns with that bias.

## 4.7 Worked Example

Suppose XAUUSD's Daily chart shows: swing low at $2,300 → swing high at $2,380 (HH) → pullback to $2,340 (HL, above the prior low) → new swing high at $2,410 (HH). This is a clear uptrend (HH, HL sequence). If price later breaks below $2,340 (the last HL), that would constitute an MSS, warning you that the uptrend may be over and a new downtrend or range could be forming — directly changing how you'd treat any resistance zone above current price.

## 4.8 Chapter Summary

- Market structure must be established before marking or trading any SNR zone.
- Swing highs/lows are labeled HH, HL, LH, LL to classify trend as up, down, or ranging.
- BOS confirms trend continuation; MSS warns of a potential reversal.
- HTF structure sets directional bias; LTF structure times entries — the foundation of multi-timeframe analysis (Chapter 11).
- SNR zones aligned with the prevailing trend generally carry higher probability than counter-trend zones.
