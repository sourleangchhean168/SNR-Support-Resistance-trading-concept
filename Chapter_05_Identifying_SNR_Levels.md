# Chapter 5: Identifying SNR Levels

## 5.1 From Theory to Mechanics

Chapters 1–4 built the conceptual foundation: why price moves, how to read candles, what SNR means, and how market structure frames it. This chapter turns that theory into a repeatable, mechanical process for marking SNR zones on any chart, for any instrument, including XAUUSD.

## 5.2 The Two-Candle Rule

The core SNR identification method used throughout this book relies on a simple two-candle sequence that marks the exact point where control shifted from one side to the other:

- **Support forms** where you see a **bearish candle immediately followed by a bullish candle**. The bearish candle represents the last gasp of selling; the bullish candle confirms buyers took over. The zone is drawn from the body of these two candles.
- **Resistance forms** where you see a **bullish candle immediately followed by a bearish candle**. The bullish candle represents the last gasp of buying; the bearish candle confirms sellers took over.

This is deliberately simple. You are not looking for complex patterns — you are looking for the literal moment of the handoff between buyers and sellers.

## 5.3 Body vs. Wick — Which Edge to Use

As introduced in Chapter 2, the zone should be drawn using the **candle bodies (open/close)**, not the wicks:

- For a support zone: draw the top of the zone at the **higher of the two candle bodies' closing/opening edges**, and the bottom of the zone at the **lowest body edge** of the two candles. Ignore the wicks below.
- For a resistance zone: mirror this — draw the bottom of the zone at the lower body edge, and the top at the higher body edge. Ignore the wicks above.

Why ignore wicks? A wick represents price that was **rejected** — orders that existed briefly and were overwhelmed. The body represents price that was **accepted** — where the candle actually settled. Since SNR zones are about where real, sustained order flow existed, the body is the more reliable reference.

## 5.4 Step-by-Step Marking Process

1. Switch to your analysis timeframe (Daily or H4, as established in Chapter 2).
2. Scan backward from the current candle, identifying clear directional impulses (fast, large-bodied moves).
3. At the origin of each impulse, locate the two-candle handoff sequence described in Section 5.2.
4. Draw a rectangle (zone) using the body-based rule from Section 5.3 — not a single line.
5. Label the zone as support or resistance, and note the date/timeframe it formed on.
6. Repeat for the 3–5 most significant zones currently visible — resist the urge to mark every minor reversal (this is addressed further in Chapter 26).

## 5.5 Filtering for Significance

Not every two-candle handoff deserves a zone. Apply these filters before marking a level as tradable:

- **Impulse size** — the move away from the zone should be clearly larger than average recent candles; a weak, slow departure suggests a low-quality zone (as noted in Chapter 3).
- **Structural relevance** — the zone should align with a swing high/low or MSS from Chapter 4. Zones that form in the "middle of nowhere," unrelated to structure, are lower priority.
- **Timeframe weight** — a Daily/H4 zone always outweighs an M15 zone at a similar price, per Chapter 2's timeframe hierarchy.

## 5.6 Worked Example: Marking XAUUSD Support

Imagine on the XAUUSD H4 chart you see: a bearish candle closing at $2,362, followed immediately by a strong bullish candle opening at $2,360 and closing at $2,378, after which price rallies to $2,410. Applying the rule:

- The bearish→bullish handoff at $2,360–$2,362 marks a support zone.
- The zone's boundaries: top at $2,362 (bearish candle's close), bottom at $2,360 (bullish candle's open) — a tight, precise zone rather than a wide guess.
- Because the subsequent rally to $2,410 was fast and large, this zone passes the impulse-size filter and is worth marking as a significant support level for future retests.

## 5.7 Common Marking Mistakes

- **Using wicks instead of bodies** — creates zones that are too wide and imprecise, generating false signals.
- **Marking every minor pullback** — clutters the chart and dilutes focus from the truly significant zones (see Chapter 26 for a full breakdown of chart-clutter mistakes).
- **Ignoring timeframe context** — marking an M15 zone and trading it with the same conviction as a Daily zone.
- **Marking zones that ignore structure** — a "textbook" two-candle handoff that occurs randomly mid-range, unrelated to any swing point, is usually low quality.

## 5.8 Chapter Summary

- SNR zones are identified using a two-candle handoff: bearish→bullish for support, bullish→bearish for resistance.
- Zones are drawn from candle bodies, not wicks, because bodies represent accepted price.
- Significant zones align with strong impulses and existing market structure (swing points, MSS).
- Daily/H4 zones are prioritized over lower-timeframe zones for reliability.
