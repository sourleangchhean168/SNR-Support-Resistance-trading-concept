# Chapter 17: Reversal and Continuation Patterns

## 17.1 Patterns Are Confirmation, Not Predictions

Candlestick and price-action patterns are useful only when they appear at a meaningful SNR zone and agree with market structure. A bullish engulfing candle in the middle of a range has far less value than the same pattern at a fresh support zone aligned with an uptrend. This chapter organizes the most useful reversal and continuation patterns for the SNR framework without treating any pattern as a guaranteed signal.

## 17.2 Reversal Patterns at SNR

A reversal pattern suggests that the current move may be losing control and the opposing side is taking over.

### Bullish Reversal Patterns

- **Bullish engulfing** — a bullish candle fully covers the prior bearish candle's body, showing a decisive shift toward buyers.
- **Hammer or bullish pin bar** — price trades substantially lower but closes back near the upper part of the candle, showing rejection of lower prices.
- **Morning-star structure** — a bearish candle, a small indecision candle, and a strong bullish candle; most useful at major support rather than in isolation.
- **Double-bottom reaction** — price tests a support area twice and fails to break it, especially when the second test includes a liquidity sweep and bullish structure shift.

### Bearish Reversal Patterns

- **Bearish engulfing** — a bearish candle fully covers the prior bullish candle's body.
- **Shooting star or bearish pin bar** — price trades higher but closes back near the lower part of the candle, showing rejection of higher prices.
- **Evening-star structure** — a bullish candle, an indecision candle, and a strong bearish candle.
- **Double-top reaction** — price tests resistance twice and fails, particularly when the second test takes buy-side liquidity before reversing.

## 17.3 Continuation Patterns at SNR

Continuation patterns suggest that a pullback is ending and the existing trend may resume:

- **Flag or channel pullback** — price corrects in a controlled channel toward an SNR zone, then breaks back in the trend direction.
- **Inside-bar breakout** — a smaller candle forms within the prior candle's range, followed by a breakout aligned with the trend.
- **Consolidation breakout** — price pauses near a zone, absorbs opposing orders, and then expands in the prevailing direction.
- **Higher-low or lower-high continuation** — a structural pullback forms an HL in an uptrend or LH in a downtrend, followed by a break of the corrective swing.

A continuation pattern should not be traded simply because it resembles a flag or inside bar. It must occur within the MSNR and MTF context defined in Chapters 10–11.

## 17.4 The Quasimodo-Style Reversal

A Quasimodo-style reversal is a structural pattern involving a final higher high followed by a lower low in a prior uptrend, or a final lower low followed by a higher high in a prior downtrend. Traders often use the resulting opposing swing area as an entry zone.

Within this book's framework, the pattern is valid only when:

1. A meaningful HTF SNR zone is nearby.
2. A clear MSS confirms that control may have shifted.
3. Price returns to the relevant zone and produces an LTF trigger.
4. The stop is placed beyond the invalidation swing, not at an arbitrary distance.

## 17.5 Second-Movement Confirmation

The first reaction from an SNR zone can be too aggressive or ambiguous to enter safely. A second movement offers additional information:

- Price reacts from the zone.
- It makes a small corrective move.
- It returns to test the reaction area or forms a higher low/lower high.
- The market then breaks the corrective swing in the intended direction.

This approach sacrifices some entry precision but may reduce false signals by requiring evidence that the first reaction was not merely temporary volatility.

## 17.6 Ranking Pattern Quality

Rank a pattern by context rather than appearance alone:

- **Highest quality** — pattern at a fresh HTF zone, aligned with MSNR, supported by a liquidity sweep or MSS, and confirmed on the LTF.
- **Moderate quality** — pattern at a valid zone with trend alignment but limited additional confluence.
- **Low quality** — pattern in the middle of a range, against HTF structure, or without a meaningful zone.

Never increase risk merely because a candle pattern looks visually dramatic. Pattern quality is contextual, and the risk framework in Chapter 19 remains the final authority.

## 17.7 Worked Example: Bullish Reversal at Support

XAUUSD Daily is in an uptrend and pulls back into a fresh support zone at $2,370–$2,378. On M15, price sweeps below $2,370, closes back inside the zone, and forms a bullish engulfing candle. A small pullback creates a higher low, followed by a break above the reaction high.

The setup contains a fresh SNR zone, HTF trend alignment, a sell-side liquidity sweep, bullish engulfing confirmation, and a second-movement higher-low confirmation. Entry occurs after the corrective high breaks, with invalidation below the sweep low and targets at the next structural resistance areas.

## 17.8 Common Pattern Mistakes

- Trading every engulfing candle regardless of location.
- Confusing a large candle with a confirmed reversal.
- Drawing a pattern after the fact without defining the rules beforehand.
- Entering before the pattern closes.
- Ignoring the difference between a reversal pattern and a continuation pattern.
- Using too many pattern names instead of maintaining a small, testable playbook.

## 17.9 Chapter Summary

- Patterns confirm context; they do not predict price independently.
- Reversal patterns are most useful at meaningful support or resistance with structural alignment.
- Continuation patterns should occur during a pullback toward an SNR zone and resolve in the prevailing trend direction.
- Quasimodo-style and second-movement setups require structural confirmation and clearly defined invalidation.
- A small, rules-based pattern library is more useful than memorizing dozens of formations.
