# Chapter 9: Role Reversal Levels

## 9.1 Closing Out Part 2

This chapter concludes Part 2 by formalizing a concept referenced since Chapter 3: when a support or resistance zone breaks, it frequently flips roles and becomes the opposite type of zone. This is one of the most reliable and widely used SNR concepts, often abbreviated **SBR** (Support-Become-Resistance) and **RBS** (Resistance-Become-Support).

## 9.2 Why Role Reversal Happens

Recall from Section 3.2 that support and resistance are the same underlying mechanism (order imbalance) viewed from opposite directions. When a support zone finally breaks:

- Traders who bought at that support and held through the breakdown are now underwater. When price rallies back up to retest the broken zone from below, many of these trapped traders sell to exit at breakeven — recreating selling pressure at the same price that previously attracted buyers. This converts old support into new resistance (SBR).
- Simultaneously, new sellers who missed the initial breakdown see the retest as a second chance to enter short, adding further selling pressure.

The mirror logic applies to RBS: traders who sold at a resistance zone that later breaks become trapped shorts, buying back at breakeven when price retests from above, converting old resistance into new support.

## 9.3 Identifying a Valid Role Reversal Setup

1. Identify a broken SNR zone — confirmed by a clear, decisive candle close beyond the zone (not just a brief wick poke through it).
2. Wait for price to retrace back toward the broken zone.
3. Watch for a reaction (rejection candles, the two-candle handoff pattern from Chapter 5) as price re-tests the zone from the opposite side.
4. Confirm the retest aligns with current market structure (Chapter 4) — a role-reversal setup that fights a strong HTF trend is lower probability than one that aligns with it.

## 9.4 The "Retest" Is Not Guaranteed

A critical caveat: not every broken zone gets retested, and not every retest holds. Price sometimes breaks and simply continues without ever looking back (especially during strong impulsive moves or major news events). Role reversal is a **conditional** setup — you wait for the retest to actually happen and show a reaction before treating it as a trade opportunity; you never assume it in advance and pre-place large orders purely on the expectation of a retest.

## 9.5 Role Reversal and the Freshness Rule

Chapter 7 established that repeated testing depletes a zone's strength. Role reversal is one of the two named exceptions to that rule (Section 7.4), because breaking the zone fundamentally changes the participants and motivations involved — it's no longer the same order flow, but a new set of trapped/breakeven-seeking traders. This means a role-reversal retest can act with nearly the same strength as a fresh zone, even though the price level itself has technically been "touched" before.

## 9.6 Trading Role Reversal Zones

Role reversal setups fit naturally into the entry frameworks detailed in Part 4 (Chapters 14–15), but the key distinguishing entry trigger is:

- Wait for the break and close beyond the original zone.
- Wait for price to return into the broken zone.
- Look for a rejection candle or two-candle handoff confirming the role reversal (e.g., a bullish-then-bearish handoff at a former support-turned-resistance zone).
- Enter in the new direction, with a stop placed beyond the zone's outer boundary (from Chapter 8's zone-construction rules).

## 9.7 Worked Example

Suppose XAUUSD support at $2,320–$2,325 breaks decisively, with price closing at $2,310. Two days later, price rallies back up to $2,322, forms a bullish candle followed by a bearish rejection candle (the resistance handoff pattern from Chapter 5) right at the old zone, and turns back down toward $2,290. This is a textbook SBR role reversal: the former support at $2,320–$2,325 is now acting as resistance, confirmed by both the retest reaction and the two-candle handoff.

## 9.8 Chapter Summary

- Broken support often becomes resistance (SBR), and broken resistance often becomes support (RBS), because trapped traders create new opposing pressure at the same price.
- A valid role-reversal setup requires a decisive break, a retest, and a confirmed reaction — never assume the retest in advance.
- Role reversal is an exception to the freshness-depletion rule from Chapter 7, since the underlying order flow has fundamentally changed.
- Entries should be triggered only after visible confirmation (rejection candle or two-candle handoff) at the retest.
