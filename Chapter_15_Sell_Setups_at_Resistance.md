# Chapter 15: Sell Setups at Resistance

## 15.1 The Mirror Framework

Chapter 14 covered buying at qualified support. This chapter applies the same analytical framework in reverse to selling at a qualified resistance zone. The direction changes, but the principles remain identical: establish structure, validate the zone, wait for price to arrive, require a trigger, define invalidation, and target logical opposing liquidity.

## 15.2 Pre-Entry Checklist

Before considering a sell setup, confirm every item below:

1. **HTF structure** (Chapter 4) shows a downtrend (LH/LL) or the setup is at the top of a confirmed range (Chapter 10.6).
2. **Zone is properly constructed** (Chapters 5, 8) using the bullish-to-bearish handoff and body-based boundaries.
3. **Zone passes the MSNR filter** (Chapter 10) — aligned with structure, not counter-trend.
4. **Zone is fresh or, at most, once-tested** (Chapter 7), unless it's a qualifying role-reversal zone (Chapter 9).
5. **MTF confirms a corrective rally**, not an MSS invalidating the downtrend (Chapter 11).
6. **At least one confluence factor** is present from Chapters 12–13: trendline, bearish candle pattern, liquidity sweep, FVG, or premium-zone location.

## 15.3 Entry Trigger

Once price enters the qualified resistance zone on the LTF chart (M15/M5), wait for one of these explicit triggers:

- **Two-candle handoff** — bullish candle followed by a decisive bearish candle inside the zone.
- **Rejection candle** — shooting star or bearish pin bar with a long upper wick that closes back below the zone's upper boundary.
- **Liquidity sweep reversal** — price briefly spikes above the zone to collect buy-side liquidity, then closes sharply back inside it.

The rule is identical to Chapter 14: reaching the zone is not an entry signal. You need evidence that sellers have actually taken control.

## 15.4 Stop-Loss Placement

Place the stop just beyond the zone's outer boundary:

- For a standard resistance zone, place it above the outer boundary with a volatility-appropriate buffer based on ATR (Chapter 8.5).
- For a setup involving a liquidity sweep, place it above the sweep high — the point beyond which the trade thesis is invalidated.

Never place a stop exactly on the zone boundary. A small buffer protects against normal spread, slippage, and wick penetration without invalidating the underlying setup.

## 15.5 Take-Profit Targets

Use the next significant downside structural points as targets:

- **First target** — the most recent swing low (Chapter 4), where buying pressure may return.
- **Second target** — the next significant HTF support zone.
- Take partial profit at the first target and trail the remainder toward the second when the market confirms continuation.

## 15.6 Position Sizing

Apply the same confluence-based sizing framework from Chapter 14:

- **0–1 additional confluence points** — minimum size or skip.
- **2 points** — standard planned risk.
- **3+ points** — maximum planned risk within your predefined account-risk limit, never an excuse to exceed it.

A strong setup does not justify risking more than your trading plan allows. Confluence improves selection; it does not eliminate uncertainty.

## 15.8 When Not to Sell Resistance

Skip the setup when any of the following conditions applies:

- HTF structure is strongly bullish and no meaningful MSS has appeared.
- Price enters the zone with an exceptionally strong bullish impulse and no rejection.
- The zone has been tested repeatedly and lacks fresh confluence.
- The required stop would be too wide for the planned risk or would produce an unrealistic target distance.
- A major scheduled news event is imminent and your plan does not explicitly accommodate its volatility.

## 15.9 Chapter Summary

- Sell setups follow the same logic as buy setups, mirrored around a resistance zone.
- A valid short requires bearish HTF structure or a range-top context, a quality zone, MTF alignment, and a clear LTF trigger.
- Stops go beyond the zone's outer boundary or liquidity-sweep high; targets reference swing lows and HTF support.
- Confluence increases setup quality but never removes risk or permits exceeding your account-risk limit.
