# Chapter 2: Chart Reading Basics

## 2.1 The Three Main Chart Types

Before you can mark a single SNR zone, you need to read a chart fluently. There are three chart types every trader encounters:

- **Line charts** — plot only the closing price of each period, connected by a line. Useful for spotting the overall trend at a glance, and used in the Malaysian SNR method (Chapter 6) to draw clean "V" and "A" shapes.
- **Bar charts** — show open, high, low, and close (OHLC) as vertical bars with small tick marks. Rarely used by retail traders today but still common in some institutional software.
- **Candlestick charts** — the standard for modern trading. Each candle displays open, high, low, and close in a visual format that instantly shows who won the battle between buyers and sellers during that period.

For SNR trading, you will primarily work with **candlestick charts** to identify precise zones, and occasionally switch to a **line chart overlay** to apply the Malaysian SNR drawing method.

## 2.2 Anatomy of a Candlestick

Every candlestick has four data points:

- **Open** — the price at the start of the period.
- **Close** — the price at the end of the period.
- **High** — the highest price reached during the period.
- **Low** — the lowest price reached during the period.

The **body** (thick part) represents the range between open and close. The **wicks** (thin lines above/below the body) represent the high and low — the extremes price reached but didn't hold.

- A **bullish candle** (usually green or white) closes above where it opened — buyers won.
- A **bearish candle** (usually red or black) closes below where it opened — sellers won.

This distinction between body and wick is critical for SNR marking: in Chapter 5, you'll learn that SNR zones are drawn from the **body** (open/close), not the wick, because the wick represents rejected price, not accepted price.

## 2.3 Reading Candle Size and Shape

The size and shape of a candle tells a story about market conviction:

- **Large-bodied candles** — strong conviction; one side dominated clearly. These often mark the start of impulsive moves away from SNR zones.
- **Small-bodied candles (dojis, spinning tops)** — indecision; buyers and sellers fought to a near-draw. These frequently appear right at SNR zones, signaling exhaustion of the prior move.
- **Long wicks** — rejection. A long upper wick means price tried to go higher but was pushed back down (bearish rejection); a long lower wick means the opposite (bullish rejection). Long wicks at a level are one of the earliest visual clues you're looking at a real SNR zone.

## 2.4 Timeframes and Why They Matter

The same price chart looks completely different depending on the timeframe you view it on. A "resistance zone" on the M15 chart might be irrelevant noise on the Daily chart, and vice versa. As introduced in Chapter 1, this book uses a top-down approach:

- **Daily / H4** — used to identify the *major* SNR zones that matter most (Chapter 5).
- **H1** — used to refine zones and watch for confluence (Chapter 12).
- **M15 / M5** — used only for fine-tuning entries once a HTF zone is confirmed (Chapter 11: Multi-Timeframe Analysis).

A common beginner mistake is marking SNR zones on a 1-minute chart and expecting the same reliability as a Daily zone — the reactions simply won't be as strong, because fewer large orders are concentrated there.

## 2.5 Reading Market Structure on the Chart

Alongside candlesticks, you need to read the "skeleton" of price movement — its structure:

- **Swing high** — a candle with lower highs on both sides of it.
- **Swing low** — a candle with higher lows on both sides of it.
- An **uptrend** is a series of higher swing highs and higher swing lows (HH, HL).
- A **downtrend** is a series of lower swing highs and lower swing lows (LH, LL).
- A **range** occurs when swing highs and lows stay roughly level, oscillating between a ceiling and a floor.

SNR zones are most reliable when they form at these swing points, because a swing high/low is, by definition, the exact point where buyers or sellers took control — which is precisely what a valid resistance or support zone represents.

## 2.6 Practical Chart-Reading Drill

Before moving to Chapter 3, practice this drill on any chart (XAUUSD Daily is a good start given your trading focus):

1. Identify the last five swing highs and swing lows.
2. Label the current trend as up, down, or ranging.
3. Find the three largest-bodied candles on the visible chart and note whether they occurred near a prior swing point.
4. Find any candles with wicks at least twice the size of their body — mark them, as these are early candidates for SNR zones covered next chapter.

## 2.7 Chapter Summary

- Candlestick charts are the primary tool for SNR analysis; line charts are used specifically for the Malaysian SNR drawing method.
- A candle's body (open/close) matters more than its wick for marking SNR zones, but long wicks are useful clues of rejection.
- Timeframe choice changes the significance of any support/resistance level — always start from Daily/H4 for major zones.
- Market structure (swing highs/lows, HH/HL/LH/LL) provides the skeleton that SNR zones attach to.
